Your Digital Life Doesn't End When You Do

Bank accounts, photos, passwords, smart home devices, and even AI conversations — they all need a plan. Here's how to organize your digital estate so your loved ones aren't left locked out or scrambling.

What Is Digital Estate Planning?

It's the process of organizing your online accounts, digital assets, and instructions so the right people can access — or close — them after you're gone.

📧 Email & Communication

Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo, AOL — often the key to resetting every other account.

💰 Financial Accounts

Banking, investments, PayPal, Venmo, credit cards, and cryptocurrency.

📱 Social Media

Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X — and how you want each handled.

☁️ Cloud Storage

Google Drive, iCloud, Dropbox, OneDrive — where your photos and documents live.

🏠 Smart Home & IoT

Smart locks, Ring, Nest, WiFi — the devices that control physical access to your home.

🤖 AI Accounts

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — new accounts with personal data and no legacy policies.

Why This Matters Now

The average person has 100+ online accounts. Without a plan, your family may spend months — or years — trying to untangle your digital life, while scammers target your unused accounts.
  • Identity theft after death is rising. Scammers monitor obituaries and death records to take over dormant accounts.
  • Passkeys are replacing passwords. You can't write a passkey on paper. The old methods don't work anymore.
  • Smart home devices lock people out. Without access to your Ring, Nest, or smart lock accounts, your family may not be able to enter your home.
  • Most states now have digital asset laws (RUFADAA). But these laws are limited — executors often only get a list of accounts, not the contents inside them.
  • AI services have no death policies yet. Your ChatGPT history, custom GPTs, and subscriptions are in limbo.

The 5 Steps

You don't have to do everything at once. Start with Step 1 and work your way through.

1. Make an Inventory

List every account, device, and subscription. We provide a free template to get started.

2. Choose a Digital Executor

Pick someone tech-savvy and trustworthy. Make sure they can get into your phone and password manager.

3. Secure Your Credentials

Use a password manager with emergency access. Understand how passkeys change the game.

4. Set Up Platform Legacy Features

Google, Apple, Facebook, and Microsoft all have tools. Turn them on now.

5. Put It in Your Will

Your digital executor and wishes should be documented legally.
